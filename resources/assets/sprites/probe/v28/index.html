Auditorium furniture — stool and lectern

Window #2 kept. The room is that render, untouched; the furniture is two free-standing sprites stamped into it. The stools face the window, so the audience is seen from behind; the lectern stands to the right of the window with the speaker behind it, facing us. Both are in the foreground layer, so a figure at a seat is drawn behind its stool.

Fourteen seats on a 23 px pitch, running from the left wall to the lectern's elbow at x 340 — the stool is 15 wide, so the row is nearly shoulder to shoulder. And the broken screen is distorted, not merely cracked: see the last two sections.

The two sprites

Drawn by hand (tools/props.mjs), not generated — see the last section for why. Both sit on their own bottom row, so placing one is "put its last row on the floor line" and nothing needs an anchor. Their six colours are all colours the room render already contains, so stamping them does not widen its palette by a single entry.

auditorium-stool15×11 Backless on purpose. The audience sits with its back to us looking at the window. In the room, with people behind them

The render, the figure layer, and the foreground cut, in that order — exactly the three layers the game draws. Toggle the figures to see the cut doing its job: the stool legs and the lectern board stay in front.

All four states

The props are drawn once, in the lit room's colours, and mapped into each other state by what that state's own render does to every colour of the lit one — the same measurement dim.py makes, then snapped to that state's palette. So the unpowered stools are unpowered-dark rather than a lit sticker, and no state gained a colour.

The broken screen

The back wall is a screen, so a broken one should not be cracked glass over a perfect picture — the picture itself should be coming apart. tools/glitchScreen.mjs tears the 274×58 rectangle inside the frame into horizontal bands and damages each one: most stay intact, a quarter slip sideways, and the rest drop out, come back in the wrong colours, or repeat a band from elsewhere, with two blown scanlines across it.

Computed rather than generated, for the reason the dim frames are: every colour it writes is a colour already inside that rectangle, so the render keeps its palette exactly — 38 colours before the tear and 38 after. It reads the screen out of the untouched -clean render and writes only that rectangle back, so it is idempotent and leaves the stamped props alone.

The proportions were the whole job. The first pass corrupted about half the rows and the landscape stopped being recognisable — the panel read as noise, which says "off", not "broken". Pulled back to a quarter, the dead tree and the horizon still read underneath the damage.

Why the props are hand-drawn

Three inpaint attempts went in first, straight into the render so they would come back at the room's own scale and palette. They are kept here because what is wrong with them is the reason for the change of method.

AttemptWhat came backVerdict
lectern, 30×29 maskA plain narrow table, flat top, no slant.Wrong object
stool, 22×14 maskNothing legible — too few pixels to put a silhouette in.Unusable
lectern, 32×33 maskGenuinely good — slanted board, brass lip, correct palette and light. But it is 32 px tall, so its board sits at row 58 and swallows the speaker to the chin: a 38 px figure standing at row 93 has its head top at row 55, leaving 7 px of head above the lectern. Right object, wrong scale

That last one is the whole argument. The generator draws to fill the mask it is given, and the mask has a floor of about 32 px before it has enough room to draw furniture at all — which is taller than the furniture in this room is allowed to be. At 15×11 and 19×22 there is no generation to do: the silhouette is the sprite.

What is still open

  • The figures stand, they do not sit. There is no seated pose, so a person on a seat reads as standing behind a stool. Fixing it properly means a sit clip per role; say the word and I will generate one for resident first and see how it reads.
  • The auditorium is staffed 2, so only two of the nine seats are ever filled — the speaker at the lectern and one person in the audience. Whether an audience should gather here is a simulation question, not an art one.
  • <room>-<state>-clean.png now sits beside each render: the generator's output before stamping. It is what makes placeProps idempotent, but it does mean four more files in the tree.
