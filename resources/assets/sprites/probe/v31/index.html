Stock bars, and the resource card

Round two on the resource icons: the bars under them now have a frame and a length, the inspector shows the same bars as a little chart, water is blue, and every icon opens a card when hovered.

Everything on this page is the game's own code. It links the real stylesheets, runs a real Shaft headlessly in this tab for 300 ticks, and then mounts the real inspector and the real resource card (ui/components/resourceTip.js) against it. Hover any icon on this page — in the row below, on the plates, in the inspectors — and the card that opens is the one the game opens, with that run's numbers. Reload for a fresh run.

2 · The plate bars

Each bar is now three rects: an edge one shade darker than the fill, a dark channel, and the fill. Only the fill blinks, so an empty bin's bar keeps its shape at the bottom of the blink. A low bin's fill is as long as the share still held, on the scale of the whole bin — and since "low" means under a third, a low bar is at most 5 of its 14 units. An empty bin stays full length: it is the alarm, and has to be seen. Rooms drawn at 2×; under each, the plate alone at 7×.

Draining

One bin running down on a loop: full-length amber at the mark, shortening a unit at a time, then red.

3 · In the inspector

The real inspector, mounted on rooms from this run, with a few bins nudged so every band shows. Under each count, a bar of how full the bin is — together a small bar chart of the building. Green is fine, amber is low (an input) or filling (an output), red is empty or full. A storehouse's bars are each good's share of the whole store, and always green. These bars do not blink: the panel is read on purpose, and the shaft's plates are what catch the eye.

4 · The card

Six of them held open, as rendered at tick 300. The name and kind; a description; a line from somebody who lives with it; the live totals; then who makes it and who uses it, busiest first, with how many are built and what each is doing right now. Buildings that could make it but are not built are listed as none built, because that is part of the answer.

5 · Water, in blue

The Nexus-only rule for blue is gone from the style guide (principles §6). Water has its own ramp in tokens.css, --water-deep / --water / --water-lit / --water-glint, and the droplet is redrawn in it.

6 · Decisions worth arguing with

  • An empty bar is full length, not zero. Drawn at its true length an empty bin would have no bar at all, and the red would be one pixel of edge. Out is the alarm; it gets the whole bar.
  • The low bar's scale is the whole bin. So a low bar is short by definition (≤5 of 14 units). The alternative, scaling from the low mark down, would make "just under a third" look like a full bar and read as fine.
  • Rates are a moving average over one shift (stores.rateWindowTicks, 20 ticks), shown per day (60 ticks). A smelter makes a billet every six ticks; per-tick numbers would flicker between 0 and 6.
  • Hauling is not production. A porter moving food makes none. The ledger records only what is made or used up: buildings, people eating, repairs, construction, spoilage, deliveries and events.
  • The canteen shows up as a food user beside People. That is the sim, not the card: a canteen has its own consumes: food 1 on top of the meals it serves.
  • No delay on the card. It opens the moment the pointer is on an icon, per the request. On the shaft this means sweeping the mouse over a plate flashes cards; a 150 ms delay would stop that if it bothers you.
  • The plate's icons take the pointer; the rest of the plate does not. Clicking an icon still selects the room under it.

7 · Still open

  • Touch has no hover, so the card cannot open on a touchscreen yet. A long-press would be the natural way.
  • Power, labour and air have no per-building ledger; their cards read the network state directly (kW drawn, crews assigned, rooms scrubbing). Good enough to answer "who", not "how much, averaged".
  • The dashboard's Stores rows are still text. They could carry the icon (and so the card) for free.
