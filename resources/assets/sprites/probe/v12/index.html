Room envelope test — option 1 vs option 2

How do we get rooms that are flat side views, with slanted side walls, a floor line at row 88 and no people — and still full of stuff? Prompting alone could not do it (v11). Two routes were tested on the same rooms: the generator, the house and the council chamber. Eastward style, palette v6/03, state on.

Both options work. Both give flat side views with the envelope held and the floor on row 88 ±2.

Option 2 (inpaint in a locked shell) gives the best rooms: dense, detailed and exactly in the envelope. It costs ~20 generations per render, does not force the palette (57 and 29 colours came back, snapped to 16 afterwards) and costs 20× more per state variant.

Option 1 (blockout guide) costs 1 generation and gives clean, flat, readable rooms, but they are simpler and more literal to the blockout. Strength is a per-room dial: 70 suited the house, 50 the generator and council chamber.

Suggested workflow: sketch a blockout per building and iterate cheaply with option 1. Once the layout is approved, spend option 2 on the master on render only, then make off / broken from it with a 1-generation image-to-image pass (spec §2.3).

Option 1 — blockout guide as the start image (1 generation each)

tools/roomGuide.mjs <width> room <layout> draws the envelope (ceiling band, bevelled side walls, floor band at rows 88–95) plus the building's main masses as flat shapes. That guide is the pixflux init_image. Low strength = more freedom, high = closer to the sketch.

Option 2 — inpaint inside a locked envelope (~20 generations each)

The empty envelope is the image; only the interior (x 7 to width−8, rows 10–87) is masked, so the ceiling, side walls and floor band cannot move. inpaint_image takes no palette, so the result is snapped to palette 03 afterwards (nearest colour, no dithering).

With figures — spec §4.2 figures on each room's floor line

The same 22-px figures in every room, standing on the measured floorY. Two things to watch: the teal engineer disappears against a teal room, and the councillors are hard to find in the dark chamber. Costume colours need checking against the rooms each role works in.
