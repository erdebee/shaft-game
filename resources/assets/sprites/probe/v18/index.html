Stairwell — 2 slots, one continuous stair

The stairwell has no floors and no ceilings now: one back wall and one zigzag stair running the full height of the shaft. Each level keeps only a thin exit landing on the right, level with the rooms' floor, where people step off, plus the floor sign and a lamp beside it. Everything else is continuous.

How it stays continuous. The layout sketch repeats every 104 px (one level), and the flights run on through its edges. Each option is rendered two levels tall (128×208). seamless.py then cuts one 104-px tile out of it, placing the one unavoidable seam on the row where the two levels of the render match best, so stacked tiles read as a single stair.

Chosen: Steel A, hand-fixed by you (stairwell-user.png). It tiles cleanly, with the stair running through every join.

Round 3 (strength 120/150, one zigzag asked for explicitly): six renders; three came out as a correct continuous zigzag (120a, 150a, 120c). The other three drew separate single flights or put text on the plate. 120a is my pick: Steel A's colours with a clean pattern and an invisible join.

Earlier: Steel A was repaired at the join, but the pattern itself was off. Its two rendered floors differed slightly, so the stair broke at the join. I repainted only a 16-px band across the join with the inpaint tool; everything outside the band is pixel-identical, and the stair now runs unbroken.

Steel B (previous pick): a clean zigzag, a teal plate and lamp by every exit, and the smallest seam (difference 5.6 at the seam against about 14 for a typical row). Steel A is warmer (lamp pools, two plates) with a slightly more visible seam. The civic tries drew parallel flights with doors (A) or a double zigzag (B), so both are rejected.

Floor numbers are printed by the game on the blank plate; this page draws them with a 3×5 pixel font.

Shaft: 768 px = 128-px stairwell + 10 build slots (640 px), no lift column. Floor numbers use the 4×6 digits.
