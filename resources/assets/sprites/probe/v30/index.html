Resource icons, and the popover that shows them

One 16×16 icon per resource in the catalogue, and the plate the shaft view puts on a room that has run out of something or is about to. Icons are hand-drawn by tools/resourceIcons.mjs and listed in the asset manifest's icons array.

The popovers on this page are the real thing. This page links src/ui/styles/tokens.css and src/ui/styles/screens.css directly and builds the plates from the same markup shaftView.js builds, at the same sizes, over real room renders. Nothing here is a mock-up of the colours: change the stylesheet and this page changes with it.

1 · The popover

Shown at 2×. A plate along the top-left of the room, one icon per good, worst first. It is drawn in a layer above the foreground cuts, so a handrail or a cell door can never cover it.

2 · What the bands mean

One function decides these, bandOf in stores.js, and both the inspector's bars and this plate read it — so a red bar in the panel and a red mark on the room are the same judgement rather than two that can drift apart. The threshold is stores.lowMark, 0.33: a bin an input is drawn from goes amber below a third, and a bin an output fills goes amber above a third.

Why missing and not "empty". A smelter has a bin for all six ores. An empty silica bin is not a problem unless it was trying to melt glass, and a plate showing six red icons on a smelter happily making steel would be worse than no plate at all. flowStock and componentChain already scope missing to what the building actually wanted, so reading it makes the popover agree with the inspector by construction.

Water is on the list although it is never in a store. It is piped, so a rationed building has waterShare < 1 and nothing in a bin. The inspector said "Short of water"; the shaft view had no way to say it at all. Now it does.

3 · The set

Shown at 4×, on the plate's own face colour. Every colour in every icon is a token declared in tokens.css: palette forcing is suspended for room sprites but never was for UI, and these are read against a panel rather than against rock.

Stocks 4

Physical goods, stored and hauled by hand.

Minerals 6

Raw extractables, cut at the dig face.

Components 11

Refined and assembled. The chain from ore to part.

Flows 3

Networks, not goods. Nobody carries these, but a building can still be short of one.

Abstracts 3

Not physical, but spent. The dashboard meters them.

4 · Legibility at size

The top row is 1× — the size they actually draw at, since shaft units are sprite pixels and zoom is whole-numbered. Then 2× and 3×. Against the plate face, against the panel colour, and against bare rock, because a porter's bubble will carry these over a dark shaft.

5 · Decisions worth arguing with

  • Hand-drawn, not generated. create_image_pixflux will not accept a 16×16 canvas at all — its floor is 1024px of area — and a 32×32 generation halved is mush. At this size every one of ~200 pixels is a decision about silhouette. Same call as the stools and the lectern in spec §2.7, one size smaller.
  • Water is not blue. Cold blue is reserved for the Nexus and the outside, Chapter 2 only (principles §6). Spending the Nexus's entrance on a droplet would be exactly the mistake that section warns about, so water is drawn in --steam and --steel-pale — which is what water in a concrete cistern looks like anyway.
  • No icon is red. --critical means alarm and nothing else. A good that has run out gets a red bar and keeps its own colours; an icon that were already red could not go red.
  • Four rocks needed four silhouettes. Iron, copper, coal, gold and limestone are all "a rock", so colour alone would not separate them: iron is one blunt chunk, copper a chunk with a splinter off it, coal three rounded lumps, gold a chunk with veins cut across, limestone pale and round.
  • The plate is left-aligned. Rooms stack in a column against the stairwell, so a plate at each room's left edge lines up into a column the eye can run down. Right-aligned strands it 380 units away on a six-slot auditorium.
  • Only "out" blinks. At 16 units a colour alone does not catch the eye at the far end of a 42-level shaft. It is a [data-part] like every other ambient loop, so it stops with the clock and stops entirely under reduced motion.
  • The ! badge is gone. It said "short of something" in the corner the plate now occupies, and the plate says which. The ≡ badge for a full output store stays, and moved to the bottom-right.

6 · Still open

  • Power has an icon but never appears on a plate: an unpowered room already goes cold grey, which principles §7 calls the state signal, and a plate would say it twice. Air quality has one but is tracked per level, so it would read identically on every room of that level.
  • The abstracts — labour, focus, authority — are drawn but unused. The dashboard meters them as text; whether those meters want icons is a UI question nobody has asked yet.
  • Nothing shows on a dark or wrecked room, on purpose. A building with no power is not short of coal in any sense the player can act on. If that turns out to hide something important, the rule is one line in syncPopover.
