Foreground cuts, and the stair

Four things people should be able to stand behind: the stair rail, the cell bars, the canteen table and the school desks. Each is cut out of the room render into a second, mostly transparent sprite that the renderer draws after the figure layer.

Nothing is removed from the room render. The cut is a copy of pixels that stay exactly where they are, so an empty room looks identical and the cut can be re-aimed later without repainting anything. It also means no inpainting: lifting a rail out of the base would leave a hole, because the wall behind it was never drawn.

Made by tools/cutForeground.mjs, which measures each object off the -on render and applies the same shapes to -off, -broken and -dim, so each state's foreground carries that state's own colours. Listed in the manifest as <id>-fg with a foregroundOf pointing back at its render.

The stair path

Porters used to follow a triangle wave centred on the column: the right shape, the wrong phase and the wrong amplitude, so they crossed the stairs rather than walking them. The path below is measured off the tile's own tread highlights — a flight down to the landing on the left, a turn, a flight back down to the right, and the level's floor. Both flights are drawn at 45°, so straight lines between a handful of control points land on every step.

These figures are placed by stairWalk() from src/ui/view/interpolate.js — the function the game itself calls — so this cannot drift from what the shaft draws. Facing comes from the direction of the walk, which reverses at each landing, not from the direction of the journey.
