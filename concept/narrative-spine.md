# Narrative Spine

*Story-only companion to the design document. Mechanics appear here only where they carry story weight.*

---

## Premise

Generations after "the Collapse," a sealed underground settlement — a **Shaft**, dug ever downward from a founding excavation — houses several thousand people across dozens of levels. Nobody alive has seen the surface. What they know of the outside comes from rationed archives and a single video feed showing a poisoned world.

Society runs on a founding charter treated as sacred law, enforced by an internal order-keeping body. The Shaft believes two things about itself: that it governs itself, and that it sustains itself.

Both are false.

---

## Central theme

**Nothing is as independent as it appears.**

The story strips away one layer of that illusion per act:

1. The Mayor was never really in charge.
2. The Chair was never really in charge either.
3. The Shaft was never self-sufficient.
4. And none of this is unique to your Shaft.

The structure repeats at every scale. A council with a leader who answers to something above them — Board and Chair, Presidium and Coryphaeus. Every rung believes it is the top until it is shown otherwise.

## Chapter 1 — The Mayor

**You play:** the elected public leader. Your authority is real, ordinary, and entirely legitimate as far as anyone knows.

The chapter is mostly competence and routine. Keep people fed, keep the peace, rule on disputes. Underneath it, inconsistencies accumulate: archive records that don't reconcile, power draws matching no known system, sections of the Shaft nobody can explain.

Chasing these leads is optional in principle and irresistible in practice.

### The finale

The leads converge on the sitting Chair of the Advisory Board, caught executing something behind the Mayor's back.

The catch is real. So is the trap — the Chair knew all along.

Before killing the Mayor, the Chair confesses, acting against his own will. **The Mayor was never in control.** The Nexus has always had the final word. The Chair is as much a puppet as anyone in the Shaft.

A secret shared between exactly two people, one of whom is about to die.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Chair

**You play:** the same Chair who committed that murder. The guilt transfers with the save file.

The Board takes control after the Mayor's death and installs the Chair as interim Mayor. You now hold three roles at once — public Mayor, Chair of the Board, and the Nexus's extended arm — and the first task is quietly brutal: walk into the meeting where the death is announced and perform shock convincingly for people who have no idea.

### The deception structure

| What the Shaft believes | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| Mayor → Advisory Board → Chair, who merely facilitates | Chair → the Apocrypha → the Nexus → somewhere outside |

Only the Chair knows the Nexus exists. To everyone else, the Apocrypha occasionally yields sacred, dangerous knowledge that only the Chair is trusted to interpret.

The result is a one-person theocracy hiding inside a bureaucracy. When the Nexus issues a commandment, the Chair launders it as scripture — presenting it to the Board as a revelation drawn from the restricted archive. The Board never learns there is anything outside the Shaft at all.

The Nexus never calls it the Shaft. In its directives the place is a **Facility**, a number — a word from a filing system the Chair has no context for. It is the theme hiding in a piece of vocabulary: somewhere outside, someone keeps a list, and your home is a line on it.

### The pressure

The Board is old, and it has been paying attention for decades. Revelations that arrive too often, too conveniently, or too far out of step with the Chair's own record start to read as suspicious. The text hasn't changed in generations — so why does its meaning keep shifting to match need?

Doubt doesn't destroy the Chair. It isolates them. A skeptical board member simply stops implementing revelations in their own domain, and the Chair's control over the Shaft quietly develops holes.

### The finale — the severance attempt

1. **Reading between the lines.** Enough digging in the Apocrypha yields a theory: the Nexus can be traced, jammed, or seized.
2. **From machine to human.** The Nexus has always behaved like an automated dispatcher, speaking only in chapter and verse. Then something breaks pattern — timing that only makes sense if someone is watching live, a response carrying a flicker of irritation. Someone is on the other end. A **handler**, assigned to this Shaft, themselves a node in something larger.
3. **Building the coalition.** The Chair must decide which board members to bring into confidence — finally saying aloud what they have hidden all chapter, to people who could just as easily report them.
4. **The demonstration.** The Presidium does not negotiate. It shows: the clean water and scrubbed air the settlement believed it produced have been supplied from outside all along. The founding myth of self-reliance is false. *(Seeded from Chapter 1 — the scrubber catalyst players will have noticed they can never mine.)*
5. **The takeover.** Judging the Chair unreliable, the Presidium enters physically, through a hidden passage foreshadowed earlier as a dismissible oddity on a lower-level schematic.

### Aftermath

- The **general population never learns.** The cover story holds completely.
- The **Board survives** — forced to learn the truth and comply. They become coerced insiders, performing ignorance daily about something they can never unlearn.
- The **Chair is eliminated.**
- A board member **revealed as a long-installed Presidium mole** is publicly framed as a talented colleague and installed as the new Mayor and Chair. This retroactively explains the Presidium's apparent omniscience — not magic, an inside source. Strongest if it is the member with the *lowest* accumulated doubt: the one who seemed safest.

## Chapter 3 — The Coryphaeus

**You play:** the head of the Presidium, holding absolute authority over every Shaft.

The perspective shift is the point. You watch the events of Chapter 2 from the one seat with enough power to have stopped them, and you didn't. Your former life is erased and quietly replaced as a routine item in a log.

*Naming note: a coryphaeus led the Greek chorus — speaking for the group, setting its rhythm. Board and Chair, Presidium and Coryphaeus. The same shape, one level up.*

### Structure

Not a network to micromanage. **Episodic missions**, one Shaft at a time, each surfacing as the Presidium becomes aware of a problem. Each stresses a different subsystem so no solution is reusable:

| Shaft | Crisis |
|---|---|
| Failing geothermal | The power source is dying; transition without a blackout cascade |
| Rising groundwater | Pumping costs spiral; lower levels become unusable |
| Collapsed food chain | Blight or seed-stock failure, under a hard clock |
| Political fracture | Resources are fine. The people are not |
| **The abandoned Shaft** | Nobody is left |

### The abandoned Shaft

Its own mode, not another crisis. No population, no meters, nothing to manage. Restore power level by level and read what remains — maintenance logs, Accord amendments made in the final months, the last Chair's records, personal effects.

The player reconstructs a rebellion that **succeeded**. Then the records stop, because everyone walked out onto the surface and died there.

It is Chapter 1 in a mirror: the same institutions, the same pressures, different choices, a worse ending than the one you helped enforce. This is where the Presidium's cruelty becomes legible without being excused.

### The Great Work

The Presidium's actual motive, and the thing that complicates every judgment the player has made: pooled resources across all Shafts, building technology to clean the surface and let everyone finally leave.

Each Shaft stabilized contributes. Each Shaft **stripped for materials** contributes faster, at that Shaft's cost. Repeated across missions, that trade is the moral engine of the chapter.

Shafts degrade while unattended. Some can be lost permanently — and losing one is not a game over. At this scale, some losses are structural. A version of this chapter where everything is salvageable would undercut everything it is about.

### The knot

The structure that lied, controlled, and killed may also be the only thing capable of saving everyone. Both statements are true. Nothing in the ending resolves that.

---

## Endings

Complete the Great Work and evacuate · sabotage it · seize it for yourself · expose the network publicly · collapse through mismanagement.

---

## v1 scope

Chapters 1 and 2 are the shipping story. Chapter 3 remains vision.

**v1 ends** on a short non-interactive epilogue from the Coryphaeus's perspective: the takeover of your Shaft logged as a routine item among many, a glimpse of a wider structure on a status board. Closure on the arc the player actually played, and the hook for what lies beyond it — requiring none of Chapter 3's systems to exist.
