# Terminology Glossary

*Single reference for all project terminology. Organised by category, alphabetical within each.*

---

## World and setting

**The Collapse** — the unspecified catastrophe that drove humanity underground, generations before the game opens. Deliberately never fully explained.

**Facility** — the Presidium's administrative term for a Shaft, always paired with a number (*Facility 12*). The Nexus and the Core Mandate use no other word for the player's home; nobody who lives there has ever heard it. The distance between "the Shaft" and "Facility 12" is the whole game in two words.

**Level** — one horizontal floor of the Shaft. The unit of vertical distance, of air-quality tracking, and of build space.

**The Shaft** — a sealed underground settlement of several thousand people across dozens of levels, grown downward from a founding excavation that never stopped being dug. The word people who live there use for home; worn-in, universal, and never written in a Presidium record — see **Facility**. The player's home Shaft is the setting of Chapters 1 and 2; Chapter 3 reveals there are many.

**The surface** — the poisoned world above, known only through rationed archives and a single video feed. Whether the feed is honest is a live question.

---

## Roles and institutions

**The Advisory Board** — long-sitting council of department elders. Outlasts individual Mayors, and appoints their successors. Composed of senior representatives from Cultivation, Engineering, Order, and the Archive.

**The Chair** — leader of the Advisory Board. Publicly a meeting facilitator, a job nobody envies. Privately the Shaft's true authority and the only person who knows the Nexus exists. Playable in Chapter 2.

**The Coryphaeus** — head of the Presidium. Absolute authority over every Shaft. Playable in Chapter 3. *(A coryphaeus led the Greek chorus, speaking for the group and setting its rhythm — the same shape as Board and Chair, one level up.)*

**The handler** — the human on the far end of the Nexus, assigned to a specific Shaft. Believed by the Chair to be an automated system until late in Chapter 2. Themselves a subordinate within a larger structure.

**The Mayor** — the elected public leader of a Shaft. Genuine authority in every visible respect, and none in the ones that matter. Playable in Chapter 1.

**The mole** — a board member secretly installed by the Presidium long before the game opens. Revealed in Chapter 2's aftermath and framed publicly as the new Mayor and Chair. Explains the Presidium's apparent omniscience.

**The Presidium** — the central controlling Shaft, and the council that governs it. Unknown to every Shaft but its own. In its own records every Shaft, including itself, is a numbered **Facility**.

**The skeptic** — emergent, not scripted. Any board member whose doubt crosses a threshold and who begins actively questioning the Chair's revelations.

---

## Documents and places

**The Accord** — the Shaft's amendable body of law. Written and revised by the player; its founding text contains clauses nobody currently alive remembers.

**The Apocrypha** — the restricted rear chamber of the archive, accessible only to the Chair. Said to hold knowledge dangerous to those who might misread it. Actually holds the Nexus.

**The Core Mandate** — a large body of pre-existing doctrine. The Nexus does not usually author new orders; it points to which dormant chapter of the Mandate now applies.

**The Nexus** — the hidden channel in the Apocrypha carrying directives from the Presidium. Speaks in chapter and verse, impersonally, which is why the Chair assumes it is a machine.

---

## Political mechanics

**Authority** — political capital spent enacting Accord amendments. Regenerates between amendment windows. Controversial laws cost more; contradicting your own precedent costs more still.

**Board doubt** — Advisory Board suspicion, tracked **per member**. Does not fail the game; fails specific capabilities, as a doubting member stops implementing revelations in their own domain. Chapter 2 only.

**Case history** — the visible record of a player's accumulated leaning on a given theme.

**Codification** — the option, once a leaning is well established, to fold a pattern of rulings into permanent statute at a discounted Authority cost.

**Dilemma** — an event presenting a specific human situation and three or four rulings. Bundles systemic response and personal judgment into a single choice. Hard-pauses the simulation.

**Flip-flop penalty** — the Stability and Trust cost of repealing a law, scaled to how long it stood.

**Leaning** — the direction a player's rulings trend on a given theme (scarcity, dissent, health): pragmatic, harsh, or lenient. Accumulates invisibly at first, then becomes a visible case history.

**Precedent** — law made by ruling on actual cases, as opposed to statute. Raises the cost of later statutes that contradict it.

**Promise timer** — a countdown started by pledging to resolve something within a deadline. Met, it becomes a permanent Trust gain plus a "keeps promises" flag. Missed, it costs more than the harsh option would have.

**Revelation staging** — the Chapter 2 mechanic of choosing how to present a Nexus directive to the Board: faithful proclamation, reinterpreted teaching, buried footnote, or withhold.

**Statute** — law written proactively in the Accord editor, as opposed to precedent.

## Meters

**Freedom** — civil liberties. Inversely tied to control. Low Freedom angers factions and feeds unrest.

**Productivity** — economic output across the Shaft.

**Stability** — resistance to unrest. Failure state is rebellion.

**Structural integrity** — the physical soundness of the Shaft. Degrades with excavation and neglect.

**Trust** — population belief that leadership is acting in good faith. The population-facing axis throughout. Failure state is rebellion.

---

## Resources

**Flow resource** — produced and consumed continuously, delivered by a network, cannot be stockpiled. Power, air quality, and water. Failure is instant and cascading.

**Stock resource** — a physical good, stored and hauled by hand. Food, scrap, ore, wood. Failure is slow and visible in advance, which is what makes it political rather than mechanical.

**Focus** — investigation capacity, generated by pulling staff off productive work. The engine of story revelation, and always in direct competition with keeping the Shaft running.

**Labour** — available worker-shifts. Consumed by every production and haulage task.

### Materials

**Basic parts** — steel components. Bulk consumable for general maintenance.

**Electrical components** — copper and trace gold. Grid, elevators, lighting.

**Precision components** — gold and silica. Terminals, sensors, clinic equipment. Slow to produce and always the bottleneck in a crisis.

**Activated carbon** — refined from coal. Scrubber filter media, and the reason Air depends permanently on Mechanical.

**Paper** — refined from wood. Consumed by the political layer itself: Accord amendments, archives, court records, the volumes of the Apocrypha.

**Scrubber catalyst** — a component the Shaft cannot produce at any depth, publicly credited to founder-era stockpiles. Actually supplied by the Presidium. The seeded detail Chapter 2's demonstration pays off.

**Wood** — grown in a legacy grove competing with food production for Cultivation slots. Deliberately not survival-critical: furniture, ceremonial fittings, instruments, splints, and paper. Spending capacity on it is a political statement.

---

## Infrastructure

**Cistern** — per-level water buffer against pump failure.

**Condition** — a building's state of repair. Decays with use; below thresholds, efficiency drops and then breakdown events fire.

**Dig face** — the active mining frontier. Veins deplete; deeper digging costs more ventilation, more pumping, longer hauls, and greater structural risk.

**Duct network** — carries scrubbed air. Sealing a level isolates it — useful during fire, contamination, or unrest, and also the reason it begins suffocating.

**Haulage** — moving stock resources between levels by assigned porters. Stairwell (slow, free, fatiguing), freight elevator (fast, costs power, breaks), dumbwaiter (medium, small loads).

**Junction** — per-level power distribution point, with a capacity cap.

**Priority rank** — the per-building order set in advance determining what browns out first when power supply falls below demand.

**Reclamation** — greywater recovery at the base of the Shaft. Water flows down free by gravity and must be pumped back up at a power cost proportional to lift height.

**Trunk cable** — the vertical power spine. Transmission loss scales with distance travelled.

---

## Simulation and time

**Amendment window** — a discrete opening, monthly or crisis-triggered, during which the Accord can be edited. Makes law punctuation rather than a menu.

**Auto-pause** — configurable triggers (first brownout, air quality threshold) that stop the clock automatically.

**Build slot** — a fixed allowance of buildings per level. Expanding means excavating downward.

**Shift** — the base time unit. Three per day. The simulation runs continuously at adjustable speed with manual pause.

---

## Chapter 3 concepts

**The abandoned Shaft** — a mission with no population and nothing to manage. Restore power level by level and read what remains, reconstructing a rebellion that succeeded and killed everyone who walked out onto the surface.

**The Great Work** — surface-cleaning technology built from resources pooled across all Shafts. The Presidium's actual motive. Stabilising a Shaft contributes; stripping one contributes faster, at that Shaft's cost.

**Shaft profile** — the per-Shaft configuration expressing one Shaft's distinct character as a delta over the base: a dying geothermal source, a rising water table, an exhausted copper vein.
