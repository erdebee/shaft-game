# Untitled Shaft Project — Game Design Document

*Working draft. Original IP: an underground-society survival/management sim. All terminology here is original to this project.*

---

## 1. Overview

**Genre:** Survival / management simulation with narrative and political layers
**Platform:** Browser-based (HTML / CSS / JavaScript), desktop PC
**Structure:** Three chapters of escalating mechanical complexity and narrative revelation
**v1 scope:** Chapters 1 and 2 fully built. Chapter 3 documented as vision only, teased via a short epilogue.

### Premise

Generations after "the Collapse," a sealed underground settlement — a **Shaft** — houses several thousand people across dozens of levels. Nobody alive has seen the surface. What people know of the outside comes from rationed archives and a single video feed showing a poisoned world. Society runs on a founding charter treated as sacred law, enforced by an internal order-keeping body.

The player governs this Shaft. Over three chapters, they discover it was never self-governing, never self-sufficient, and never alone.

### Core theme

**Nothing is as independent as it appears.** Each chapter strips away one more layer of that illusion:

- The Mayor was never really in charge.
- The Chair was never really in charge either.
- The Shaft itself was never self-sufficient.
- And none of this is unique to your Shaft.

## 3. Core systems (all chapters)

### Resource layer
Power, water, food, air quality, structural integrity. Each tied to a department that must be staffed to function.

### Population layer
Job assignment, morale, health, aging, and a birth-lottery system capping growth to what the settlement can sustain.

### Spatial layer
The Shaft is vertical. Moving people, goods, and power between levels costs time and energy, making logistics (elevators, stairwells, pipe and cable runs) a genuine constraint rather than flavour.

### Faction layer
Four departments — **Cultivation**, **Engineering**, **Order**, **Archive** — each with its own agenda and satisfaction level. Dissatisfied factions slow output and become sabotage-prone; satisfied factions are reliable and will warn the player about brewing trouble early.

### Investigation (Focus)
A resource generated by pulling staff off productive work to dig into anomalies. Focus is the engine of story revelation, so uncovering truth always competes directly with keeping the lights on.

### Meters

| Meter | Governs | Failure state |
|---|---|---|
| **Stability** | Resistance to unrest | Rebellion |
| **Trust** | Population belief in leadership's good faith | Rebellion |
| **Freedom** | Civil liberties; inversely tied to control | Unrest, faction anger |
| **Productivity** | Economic output | Resource collapse |
| **Board doubt** (Ch. 2) | Advisory Board suspicion, tracked *per member* | Loss of departmental control |

**Trust** is the population-facing axis, present from Chapter 1. **Board doubt** is Chapter 2 only, and does not fail the game — it fails specific capabilities, as individual board members stop implementing the Chair's directives in their domain.

## 4. The law engine: statute and precedent

The Accord operates through two entry points sharing the same meters, faction logic, and consequences.

### Statute — the Accord editor
Periodic amendment windows offer a hand of law cards paid for with **Authority** (political capital). Controversial laws cost more.

| Category | Example law | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Order & security | Mandatory curfew | +stability, −freedom, −productivity |
| Order & security | Restricted zone designation | +security, −zone output |
| Economy | Rationing quotas | +resource stability, −morale |
| Economy | Overtime mandate | +productivity now, −health later |
| Freedom | Public assembly rights | +trust, −stability |
| Freedom | Petition system | +trust, +stability, creates records |
| Information | Surveillance expansion | +intel, −trust, −freedom |
| Population | Fertility lottery reform | Controls growth; reaction depends on perceived fairness |

**Repeal carries a flip-flop penalty** to Stability and Trust, scaled to how long the law stood. Early experimentation is cheap; reversing entrenched law is risky.

**Hidden clauses:** spending Focus to inspect the Accord's base text surfaces clauses buried at founding — often favouring the Presidium in ways nobody currently alive remembers. These can be exposed publicly (large Trust swing) or held as leverage.

### Precedent — event dilemmas
Random and conditional events present a specific human situation and 3–4 rulings. Each ruling bundles a systemic response *and* a judgment on the person involved, so options read as coherent stances rather than stat optimisation.

**Example — ration shortage, day 34:**
> A night-shift guard caught a cultivation hand slipping tins out through the pipe access. Under questioning he didn't deny it. His daughter has had a fever for six days, and he'd already given her his own rations twice.

| Ruling | Effect | Leaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Double the shifts** — push cultivation and mechanical to recover the shortfall; no charge | food +14, productivity −6, stability +3 | Pragmatic |
| **Cut the rations** — tighten quotas settlement-wide, make an example of him | stock security +18, trust −10, freedom −4 | Harsh |
| **Give your word** — pardon him, promise the shortage ends within a month | trust +8, starts 30-day promise timer | Lenient |

**Promise timers** are a gamble, not a free option. Meet the deadline and the Trust gain becomes permanent plus a "keeps promises" flag that strengthens future soft options. Miss it and the penalty exceeds what the harsh option would have cost, plus a "broken promise" flag that devalues all future promises.

### How the two interlock

- **Rulings build a leaning** per theme (scarcity, dissent, health) — pragmatic, harsh, or lenient — displayed as a case-history counter.
- **Precedent raises the cost of contradictory statute.** Proposing assembly rights after three harsh dissent rulings costs extra Authority and reads as hypocrisy. Consistency becomes a spendable resource.
- **Statute reshapes future dilemmas.** With rationing quotas already law, the theft event fires differently — flavour text acknowledges your own law caused the strain, and "cut the rations" may not be offered at all.
- **Codification:** once a leaning crosses a threshold (~3 consistent rulings), the next dilemma of that type offers an extra option — *make this policy* — folding the pattern into permanent statute at a discounted Authority cost, since the population already expects it.

## 5. Chapter 1 — The Mayor

**Role:** Elected public leader. Authority is real but ordinary.
**Difficulty:** Introductory. Teaches resources, population, logistics, factions, Accord basics, and dilemma rulings.
**Aesthetic:** Warm amber industrial. Concrete grey, rust, sodium lighting. Vertical cross-section view of the settlement.

### Structure
The player runs the Shaft openly and competently while an investigation thread accumulates in the background — inconsistencies in archive records, power draws matching no known system, sealed sections nobody can explain.

### Finale
The player's leads converge on the sitting Chair of the Advisory Board, caught executing something behind the Mayor's back. The catch is real — but so is the trap. The Chair knew all along.

Before killing the Mayor, the Chair confesses, acting against his own will: **the Mayor was never really in control.** The Nexus has always had the final word, and the Chair is as much a puppet as anyone. A secret shared between exactly two people, one of whom is about to die.

## 6. Chapter 2 — The Chair

**Role:** The same Chair who committed the murder. The player now inherits that guilt.
**Difficulty:** Escalating. All Chapter 1 systems continue, with hidden authority layered on top.
**Aesthetic:** Cold blue terminal layer for the Nexus; ledger and typewriter styling for the Accord editor. The Apocrypha itself is older, dustier, analogue — physical texts and sealed containers, with the Nexus as the one anachronistic, unexplained object at its centre.

### Opening
The Board takes control after the Mayor's death and installs the Chair as interim Mayor. The player now holds three roles at once: public Mayor, Chair of the Advisory Board, and the Nexus's extended arm.

The first task is quietly brutal — walk into the Board meeting where the death is announced and perform shock convincingly for people who have no idea. This doubles as a natural tutorial for the revelation-staging system, before the Nexus has sent a single directive.

### The power structure

| What the Shaft believes | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| Mayor → Advisory Board → Chair (meeting facilitator) | Chair → the Apocrypha → the Nexus → outside |

Only the Chair knows the Nexus exists. To everyone else, the Apocrypha occasionally yields sacred, dangerous knowledge that only the Chair is trusted to interpret — a one-person theocracy hiding inside a bureaucracy.

The Nexus itself never says *Shaft*. Its directives, and the Core Mandate chapters they cite, name the place a **Facility** with a number — a designation the Chair can feel the shape of but cannot place. It is the first clue, buried in vocabulary, that someone outside keeps a list.

### Revelation staging
When the Nexus issues a commandment or points to Core Mandate chapters, the Chair chooses how to present it to the Board:

| Framing | Board doubt | Presidium favour |
|---|---|---|
| **Faithful proclamation** — read near-verbatim as sacred instruction | Rises | High |
| **Reinterpreted teaching** — softened to fit Board expectations | Minimal | Partial |
| **Buried footnote** — slipped in as minor technical clause | None | Weak |
| **Withhold** — not brought to the Board at all | None | None; risks audit |

**Doubt drivers:** severity of the language, consistency with the Chair's established precedent (a harsh "revelation" after lenient rulings reads as suspicious — the text hasn't changed in decades, so why does its meaning keep shifting to match convenience?), and frequency (one proclamation a year feels sacred; one a month feels well-timed).

**Doubt consequences** are per-member. A skeptical member stops implementing revelations in their domain, freezing the Chair's control over that department. Resolution: investigate to find something reassuring, replace them, or land a revelation calibrated to what that specific member finds credible. Unmanaged, doubt escalates toward a formal inquiry demanding the Chair produce the physical text.

### Chapter 2 finale — the severance attempt

1. **Reading between the lines.** Gated behind Apocrypha investigation progress. The Chair theorises the Nexus can be traced, jammed, or seized.
2. **From machine to human.** The Nexus has always behaved like an automated dispatcher, speaking only in chapter-and-verse. Then something breaks pattern — timing that only makes sense if someone is watching live, or a response outside the usual cadence carrying a flicker of irritation or doubt. Someone is on the other end. Likely a **handler** assigned to this specific Shaft, themselves a node in a larger structure.
3. **Building the coalition.** The Chair must decide which board members to bring into confidence — finally saying out loud what they've hidden all chapter, to people who could just as easily report them. Recruiting the high-risk engineering rep grants technical muscle or ends the attempt on the spot.
4. **The attempt and the answer.** The Presidium doesn't negotiate. It demonstrates: the clean water and scrubbed air the Shaft believed it produced itself have been supplied from the Presidium Shaft all along. The founding myth of self-reliance is false.
5. **The takeover.** Judging the Chair unreliable, the Presidium enters physically through a hidden passage — foreshadowed earlier by a dismissible architectural oddity on a lower-level schematic — and takes direct control.

**Aftermath:**
- The **general population never learns** any of this. The cover story holds.
- The **Board survives**, but is forced to learn the truth and comply. They become coerced insiders, living with knowledge they must perform ignorance about daily.
- The **Chair is eliminated.**
- A board member **revealed as a long-installed Presidium mole** is framed publicly as a talented colleague and installed as the new Mayor and Chair. This retroactively explains how the Presidium always seemed to know everything — not omniscience, an inside source. Strongest if it's the member with the *lowest* accumulated doubt: the one who seemed safest.

The invasion plays out through the existing mission-and-narrative delivery system, not a bespoke mechanic — consistent with how the Mayor's death is handled.

### Epilogue (v1 ending)
A short non-interactive scene from the Coryphaeus's perspective: the takeover of your Shaft — a Facility number like any other on the board — logged as a routine item among many, a glimpse of a wider network on a status board. Closure on Chapters 1–2, plus the hook for what comes next — with no Chapter 3 systems required to build it.

## 7. Chapter 3 — The Coryphaeus (vision only, not in v1)

**Role:** Head of the Presidium, holding absolute unchecked authority over all Presidium members and all Shafts. The player watches the events of Chapter 2 from the one seat with enough power to have stopped them — and didn't.

**Aesthetic:** Schematic network map, control-room styling.

**Naming note:** a coryphaeus led the Greek chorus, speaking for the group and setting its rhythm. Board and Chair, Presidium and Coryphaeus — the same structural pattern repeating one level up, which is the whole theme of the game.

### Systems audit

**Carried over unchanged:** core resource sim for the home Shaft, population management, vertical logistics, local factions, Focus and investigation (now aimed outward at the network), precedent and leaning.

**Modified:**
- **The Accord** — authorship splits. Presidium-dictated entries are locked; only minor local matters remain editable. The codex visually distinguishes locked from editable.
- **Presidium favour** becomes **network standing** — an overt reputation stat rather than a secret currency.

**Retired:** revelation staging (nothing left to disguise at this level), Board doubt in its Chapter 2 form, the Apocrypha's secrecy function.

**New:**
- **Network map** — multiple Shaft nodes, each ticking with abstracted vitals, running semi-autonomously.
- **Shared Presidium reserves** — a finite pool allocated across nodes in crisis. Every node propped up is one not supported elsewhere.
- **Parallel crises** — one node rebelling, another facing collapse, another gone silent. Triage rather than micromanagement.
- **The Great Work** — a shared long-term resource sink: surface-cleaning technology built from pooled network resources, enabling eventual evacuation. This is the Presidium's actual motive, and the game's final moral knot — the structure that lies, controls, and kills may also be the only thing capable of saving everyone.
- **Issuing commandments** — the mirror of Chapter 2. The player now decides how much truth each subordinate Chair receives and how much leverage to hold over them, choosing whether to become exactly what broke them.

### Endings
Complete the Great Work and evacuate · sabotage it · seize it for yourself · expose the network publicly · collapse through mismanagement.

*(A Presidium council layer, making the Coryphaeus first among equals rather than absolute, is a possible later addition.)*

## 8. Look and feel

**Visual style:** 2D vertical cross-section — an "ant farm" cutaway of the full settlement. Flat, muted industrial palette: concrete grey, rust, amber sodium lighting, occasional sickly green terminal glow.

**UI:** Retro-terminal dashboard. Monospace type, analogue gauge widgets for vitals, subtle scanline texture, klaxon-style crisis alerts.

**Audio:** Low mechanical hum, echoing stairwell footsteps, muffled PA announcements, sparse tense drone rather than a full score.

**Tone:** Quiet bureaucratic dread rather than horror. Claustrophobia balanced with small human moments — a wedding, a birth, a friendship — so it doesn't collapse into unrelieved grimness.

**Per-chapter shift:** Ch. 1 warm amber industrial → Ch. 2 adds cold blue hidden-terminal layer and analogue Apocrypha → Ch. 3 opens into schematic network map.
